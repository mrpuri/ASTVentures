Why Punjab

The next GCC wave is leaving the metros.

Bengaluru, Hyderabad and Pune are saturated — rents, salaries and attrition all say so. The companies opening centres now are looking at tier-2 cities with deep graduate pools and none of the churn. Punjab is one of the strongest of them, and almost nobody has built the talent infrastructure to prove it.

That is the part we do. We already train on campuses across the state, and we already know which graduates can be made job-ready in weeks, not years.

Tier-2 cost base

Salaries, real estate and overheads well below the metros — without a drop in graduate quality.

Deep graduate pool

Engineering and business graduates from LPU, GNA, NITs and dozens of colleges, most of whom currently leave the state to find this work.

Low attrition

People stay where they're from. A centre in Punjab keeps its institutional knowledge in a way a metro centre never does.

Connected

Chandigarh airport, the Mohali IT corridor and expressway links to Delhi. Jalandhar and Ludhiana are both within reach.

How it works

From feasibility to a running team

Five stages. You can stop at any of them and take over — or keep us on for the upskilling.

1

Assess

Which functions to move, which city fits (Mohali, Jalandhar or Ludhiana), what the role mix looks like and what it costs against your current centre. You get a written feasibility note.

2

Design

The operating model, the hiring plan, the timeline and the training curriculum mapped to your actual stack and processes. Entity, real estate and compliance are scoped with our specialist partners.

3

Build the team

We recruit from our partner campuses, run a pre-hire bootcamp on your tools, assess on real work and certify. You interview a shortlist that has already been trained for the role.

4

Launch & run

Space set up, team onboarded, and our people alongside yours on site through the first ninety days. Managers from your side are supported until the centre runs itself.

5

Transfer & grow

Full handover to your leadership. Optionally keep us on for continuous upskilling, the next hiring wave and new functions as the centre expands.

Questions

What companies ask first

Do we need an Indian entity to start?

Not for a pilot pod — that runs under AST. For a full centre, yes, and our partners handle incorporation, registrations and compliance as part of the design stage. Several structures are possible; we lay out the trade-offs in the feasibility note.

Which city should we pick?

Mohali if you want an established IT corridor and airport access. Jalandhar or Ludhiana if you want the deepest graduate pool, the lowest cost base and to be the anchor employer rather than one of many. We recommend based on the functions you're moving.

How good are the graduates really?

Raw ability is comparable to anywhere in India — the gap has always been exposure and training, not talent. That gap is exactly what our bootcamps close. You interview a shortlist that has already worked on your kind of problems for several weeks.

What about senior people and managers?

A centre needs a small core of experienced leads. We source those from the Punjab diaspora in the metros — many are looking for a reason to come home — and from our own network of trainers and industry contacts.

How is this different from outsourcing?

The team is yours. Your brand, your systems, your culture, your IP — in a Build–Operate–Transfer model we hand every bit of it over on an agreed date. Outsourcing rents you capacity; a GCC builds you an asset.
